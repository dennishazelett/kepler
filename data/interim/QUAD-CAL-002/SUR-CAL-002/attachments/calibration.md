# Quadrant Calibration

Calibration characterizes the performance of the complete observer–instrument system.

The objective is not merely to verify that the instrument was constructed correctly, but to quantify how accurately and precisely it measures elevation angles under realistic observing conditions.

Every Kepler quadrant should undergo a documented calibration campaign before it is used for astronomical observations.

---

# Purpose

Calibration serves several purposes:

- verify mechanical alignment
- characterize systematic error
- estimate measurement precision
- identify construction defects
- establish instrument-specific performance

Calibration therefore measures the combined behavior of the instrument, the observer, and the measurement procedure.

---

# Measurement Philosophy

Unlike the cross-staff, which infers an angle through geometric reconstruction, the quadrant measures an angle directly.

Even so, the measured value should never be assumed correct simply because it is read from a graduated scale.

Sources of error include:

- plumb-line oscillation
- imperfect vertical reference
- graduation errors
- sight alignment
- observer reading error
- environmental conditions

Calibration estimates the combined effect of these uncertainties.

---

# Calibration Standard

The preferred calibration target should provide one or more independently known elevation angles.

Candidate approaches include:

- an indoor calibration fixture with known geometry
- a surveyed outdoor target
- comparison with a reference instrument
- astronomical reference objects

The chosen standard should provide angles known more accurately than the quadrant is expected to measure.

---

# Calibration Campaign

Each instrument should undergo a structured calibration campaign.

The initial campaign should investigate:

- repeatability
- accuracy across multiple elevation angles
- observer consistency
- environmental effects

Measurements should span the instrument's intended operating range rather than concentrating near a single angle.

---

# Calibration Survey

Calibration observations are submitted as an instrument calibration survey using the standard Kepler survey workflow.

The survey should include:

- instrument metadata
- observer information
- raw measurements
- calibration target description
- supporting notes
- photographs where appropriate

The resulting survey package becomes the canonical record of the instrument's measured performance.

---

# Acceptance Criteria

Specific numerical acceptance criteria have not yet been established.

Instead, the first prototype will be used to characterize:

- achievable precision
- systematic bias
- observer repeatability
- calibration procedure

Those results will inform future revisions of both the instrument and the calibration protocol.
