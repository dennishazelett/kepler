# Project Charter

## Mission

Kepler exists to teach that scientific knowledge is constructed through disciplined cycles of observation, measurement, modeling, inference, and communication.

The project uses naked-eye astronomy as an accessible and historically authentic laboratory in which participants experience science as a living process rather than a collection of established facts. Every observation, every instrument, every model, every line of code, and every written reflection contributes to a deeper understanding of how knowledge is created, refined, and shared.

Our objective is not simply to teach astronomy, statistics, or machine learning. Our objective is to cultivate scientific thinking.

---

# Vision

We envision a world in which anyone—regardless of age, geography, financial resources, or formal training—can participate meaningfully in authentic scientific inquiry.

A participant should be able to build simple instruments from inexpensive household materials, make careful observations of the night sky, contribute those observations to a shared body of knowledge, and explore them using methods ranging from elementary statistics to modern artificial intelligence.

The project is designed so that curiosity, careful reasoning, and thoughtful communication matter more than expensive equipment or prior expertise.

---

# Guiding Principles

## Science is an activity.

Science is not a body of facts to be memorized.

It is an iterative process of asking questions, making observations, constructing models, testing predictions, communicating results, and revising explanations.

Kepler is designed to make that process visible.

---

## Measurement comes before inference.

Every scientific conclusion begins with a measurement.

Understanding how observations are produced, calibrated, and recorded is as important as understanding the models built upon them.

The measurement process itself is therefore treated as a first-class object of study.

---

## Uncertainty is information.

Measurements are never perfect.

Observer variability, instrument limitations, environmental conditions, and incomplete data are not inconveniences to be ignored but sources of information that can themselves be studied.

Learning to reason under uncertainty is central to scientific practice.

---

## Models are tools for thinking.

Models connect hypotheses, predictions, and observations.

They are neither reality nor truth.

Different models encode different assumptions, emphasize different aspects of the data, and answer different questions.

The project encourages comparison among competing models rather than commitment to a single inferential framework.

---

## Communication is part of scientific reasoning.

Scientific understanding does not emerge solely from experimentation or computation.

Writing, discussion, visualization, and explanation are integral components of scientific thought.

Ideas become clearer through communication.

Failures become productive through reflection.

Knowledge becomes durable only when it can be shared.

Participants are therefore encouraged to document not only their conclusions but also their reasoning, uncertainties, revisions, and questions.

---

## History is a laboratory.

Scientific history is not included for context or inspiration alone.

It demonstrates how knowledge evolves.

By retracing the observational challenges faced by astronomers such as Tycho Brahe and Johannes Kepler, participants encounter the same fundamental problems of measurement, uncertainty, and inference that continue to define modern science.

History provides evidence about how scientific ideas develop.

---

## Openness accelerates discovery.

All aspects of the project—including instrument designs, protocols, software, documentation, simulation, and educational materials—should be developed as openly as practical.

Open collaboration allows ideas to be examined, improved, reproduced, and extended by others.

---

# Scope

Kepler focuses on the complete scientific measurement process.

This includes:

* building simple observational instruments;
* developing standardized observation protocols;
* collecting and curating longitudinal observational datasets;
* modeling measurement uncertainty;
* comparing statistical and machine learning approaches;
* teaching reproducible scientific workflows;
* documenting the reasoning behind scientific conclusions.

The project emphasizes methods that remain accessible to participants with minimal financial resources.

---

# Out of Scope

Kepler is not intended to become:

* professional astronomical software;
* a replacement for modern observatories;
* a platform requiring specialized or expensive equipment;
* an astronomy visualization application;
* an automated telescope control system;
* a repository of disconnected machine learning demonstrations.

Technology should lower barriers to participation rather than raise them.

---

# Audience

The initial audience consists primarily of:

* undergraduate students;
* motivated high school students with interests in STEM;
* educators seeking authentic scientific learning experiences;
* researchers interested in statistical inference, measurement, and scientific pedagogy.

The project is intentionally designed so that participants with little prior experience can contribute meaningful observations while continuing to challenge advanced learners.

---

# Success

Kepler will be considered successful if participants leave with a fundamentally different understanding of science.

Specifically, they should recognize that:

* scientific knowledge is constructed through evidence rather than received as authority;
* measurements require careful design and interpretation;
* uncertainty is unavoidable and informative;
* models embody assumptions rather than certainty;
* competing explanations can be evaluated using data;
* communication is central to scientific progress.

Secondary indicators of success include:

* a growing longitudinal observational dataset;
* widespread use in teaching statistics, machine learning, and artificial intelligence;
* contributions from an active open-source community;
* reproducible analyses that illustrate diverse approaches to inference;
* unexpected discoveries emerging from community observations.

---

# Governance

Kepler aspires to become a community-driven open-source project.

Contributions are welcomed from educators, students, scientists, historians, engineers, software developers, statisticians, and citizen scientists.

Project decisions should be guided by the principles articulated in this charter rather than by preference for any particular technology, methodology, or software ecosystem.

---

# Looking Forward

Astronomy is the first domain explored by Kepler because it combines profound scientific questions with observations that remain accessible using simple instruments.

The broader ideas developed here—measurement, uncertainty, modeling, inference, and communication—extend well beyond astronomy.

Future projects may apply the same philosophy to other scientific disciplines while preserving the principles established by this charter.

---

# Closing Statement

Every scientific discipline begins with someone paying careful attention to the world.

Kepler exists to remind us that the most powerful scientific instruments are not necessarily the most sophisticated ones.

They are the habits of careful observation, disciplined reasoning, intellectual humility, and clear communication.

Those habits are available to everyone.
