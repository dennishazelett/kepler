# Kepler Glossary Translation Guidance

This document supports translation and review of the Kepler glossary.
It records project-level conceptual distinctions that must be preserved across
language versions. It is contributor-maintenance documentation and does not
replace the public glossary or the source documents on which glossary entries
are based.

| English term | Required distinction |
|---|---|
| Observation | The durable primary record of an observational act |
| Measurement | The act or determination that produces an observation |
| Derived quantity | A later product; never a substitute for an original observation |
| Survey | A coherent package of observations and their shared context |
| Investigation | A broader participant-directed scientific inquiry |
| Protocol | A repeatable evidence-collection procedure, not a required conclusion |
| Model | An explicit tool for reasoning under assumptions |
| Inference | Reasoning from observational constraints to possible explanations |
| Uncertainty | Information to characterize, not merely error to remove |
| Reproducibility | Ability to inspect and repeat documented work; not a guarantee of identical results |

## Core distinctions

### Measurement and observation

In Kepler, a measurement is the act or result of determining a quantity.

An observation is the preserved primary record of that act or result, together
with the context needed to interpret how it was produced.

Translations must preserve this distinction. An observation is not merely a
synonym for a measurement, although ordinary-language usage may overlap.

### Survey and simulated survey

A Kepler survey is a coherent package of observations of real-world events,
collected for a shared scientific purpose under a documented observational
context. Its records include the time, place, observer, instrument, protocol,
and other relevant metadata required to preserve provenance.

A simulated survey is a synthetic dataset or survey-like package generated by
a simulation. It may model the structure of a Kepler survey, but it does not
record real-world observational events and must not be described simply as a
Kepler survey.

### Reproducibility

In Kepler, reproducibility is the capacity to inspect, understand, and repeat
a documented workflow using its recorded observations, metadata, methods,
assumptions, and computational materials.

Reproducibility does not require identical observations under changed
conditions, identical results from stochastic procedures without controlled
randomness, or identical scientific conclusions.
