# Kepler Training Program

## Purpose

The Kepler Training Program prepares participants to become competent scientific observers.

Before participating in an investigation, every participant should possess the practical skills required to produce observations that are reproducible, well documented, and scientifically useful.

The training program is intentionally separate from the scientific investigations.

Training develops observational skills.

Investigations use those skills to evaluate scientific claims.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Philosophy

The purpose of the training program is not to teach astronomy.

It is to teach the craft of scientific observation.

Participants learn how to make careful measurements, record them systematically, communicate them effectively, and contribute them to a growing body of shared scientific knowledge.

These skills are valuable regardless of the scientific claim being investigated.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Why Training Comes First

Scientific investigations depend upon reliable observations.

Before asking whether one scientific explanation is better than another, participants must first be able to answer simpler questions:

- How do I describe what I observed?
- How do I measure it?
- How do I estimate uncertainty?
- How do I communicate my observations so another person could reproduce them?

Throughout history, these questions were solved before astronomy became a predictive science.

Kepler follows the same progression.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# The Role of Measurement

The Kepler project is built upon participant-generated observations.

Every observation should be:

- carefully measured;
- accompanied by sufficient metadata;
- reproducible by another observer;
- permanently preserved.

The quality of later investigations depends directly upon the quality of these observations.

For this reason, careful measurement is treated as a scientific skill rather than a preliminary technical exercise.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# The Role of Instruments

The training program assumes that participants have access to one or more Kepler-compatible instruments.

Instrument construction, calibration, and maintenance are documented separately within the repository.

The purpose of the training program is to teach participants how to use those instruments effectively rather than how to construct them.

As instrument designs evolve, the competencies taught by the training program should remain stable.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Structure of the Training Program

Each training module follows a common structure.

## Scientific Question

The module begins with a practical problem encountered by an observer.

Participants are introduced to the scientific need that motivates the new skill.

Concepts should emerge naturally as solutions to previously encountered problems rather than being introduced as isolated topics.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Historical Context

Whenever practical, the historical development of the underlying idea is presented.

History is used not as background material but as evidence that the problems encountered by participants are the same problems faced by earlier observers.

The objective is to understand why scientific ideas were invented.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Practical Skill

Participants learn and practice the observational skill required to solve the problem.

Emphasis is placed on repeatability, careful measurement, and documentation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Reflection

Participants consider:

- what assumptions were required;
- what uncertainties remain;
- what limitations exist;
- how the new skill improves scientific observation.

Reflection is considered part of scientific practice rather than an optional activity.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Competency Check

Each module concludes with a practical demonstration of competency.

The purpose is not to assess memorization but to verify that participants can successfully apply the skill in the field.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Training Modules

The initial training program consists of five modules.

## Module 1 — Describing the Sky

**Question**

How can two independent observers communicate the location of the same object in the sky?

Participants begin by learning how to describe celestial objects using shared observational references before developing a formal coordinate system.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Module 2 — Measuring the Sky

**Question**

How can observations be transformed into quantitative measurements?

Participants learn to use Kepler instruments to measure angular relationships and estimate observational uncertainty.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Module 3 — Mapping the Sky

**Question**

How do individual measurements become a shared map of the heavens?

Participants learn how repeated measurements define a common coordinate framework and how stationary celestial objects become the reference system against which future observations are made.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Module 4 — The Moving Sky

**Question**

What changes when observations are repeated over time?

Participants begin recording the changing positions of the Sun, Moon, and wandering objects relative to the established celestial map.

The emphasis remains on description rather than explanation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Module 5 — Contributing Observations

**Question**

How do individual observations become scientific data?

Participants learn how observations are documented, validated, curated, and incorporated into the Kepler dataset so they may be reused by future investigations.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Completion

Upon completing the training program, participants should be capable of:

- producing reproducible astronomical observations;
- communicating observations using the project's measurement framework;
- documenting uncertainty and observational context;
- contributing high-quality observations to the shared Kepler dataset;
- participating fully in the scientific investigations.

Completion of the training program marks the transition from learning observational practice to conducting scientific investigations.

The investigations assume these competencies and focus on evaluating scientific claims rather than teaching observational technique.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Design Principle

Every concept introduced during training should solve a problem the participant has already encountered.

The training program therefore introduces new ideas only when they become necessary for making better observations.

Participants are not expected to memorize scientific vocabulary.

They are expected to understand why each concept was invented and how it improves the practice of science.
