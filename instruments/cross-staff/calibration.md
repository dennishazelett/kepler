# Kepler Cross-staff Calibration Guide

> **Status:** Draft
>
> This document describes the calibration and validation procedures for the Kepler Cross-staff.
>
> Calibration establishes the relationship between the physical geometry of the instrument and the angular measurements it produces.
>
> Every instrument should be calibrated before collecting scientific observations.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Purpose

The purpose of calibration is to:

- verify that the instrument functions correctly;
- quantify measurement repeatability;
- identify systematic bias;
- establish the usable measurement range;
- document the uncertainty associated with subsequent observations.

Calibration is an ongoing process rather than a one-time event.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Measurement Principle

The cross-staff measures angular separation by comparing a known fiducial width with its distance from the observer's eye.

The relationship between staff position and measured angle depends upon:

- fiducial separation;
- eye position;
- measurement geometry;
- construction tolerances.

Calibration characterizes the actual instrument rather than assuming ideal geometry.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Before Calibration

Verify that:

- the sleeve slides smoothly;
- the crosspiece remains perpendicular to the staff;
- the fiducials are secure;
- the measurement index is clearly visible.

Repair any mechanical problems before proceeding.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Stage 1 — Mechanical Validation

Measure several terrestrial objects of known width.

Examples include:

- cabinet knobs;
- books;
- rulers;
- printed calibration bars.

Record:

- object description;
- measured object width;
- distance from eye to object;
- selected fiducial;
- staff reading.

Repeat each measurement at least five times.

The objective is to evaluate repeatability rather than accuracy.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Stage 2 — Geometric Validation

For each observation, compare:

- observed staff position;
- expected staff position based upon similar triangles.

Large discrepancies should be investigated before astronomical use.

Possible causes include:

- inaccurate eye position;
- incorrect fiducial spacing;
- sleeve misalignment;
- reading error.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Stage 3 — Fiducial Characterization

Each fiducial should be evaluated independently.

Document:

- nominal separation;
- measured separation;
- practical measurement range;
- repeatability;
- ease of alignment.

Future instrument revisions may modify these fiducials based upon accumulated experience.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Stage 4 — Astronomical Validation

After successful terrestrial calibration, perform observations of celestial objects whose angular separations are well established.

Initially, these measurements should be regarded as validation exercises rather than scientific observations.

Record:

- date and time;
- observing location;
- atmospheric conditions;
- target objects;
- fiducial used;
- staff reading;
- calculated angular separation;
- accepted angular separation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Repeatability

Repeatability should be estimated by making multiple independent measurements of the same target.

Record:

- mean measurement;
- standard deviation;
- range of observations.

Repeatability is often more informative than absolute accuracy during early prototype development.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Sources of Uncertainty

Document all identifiable sources of uncertainty.

Examples include:

- eye placement;
- sleeve position;
- fiducial geometry;
- instrument flexure;
- observer judgment;
- atmospheric seeing;
- target definition.

Future improvements should attempt to reduce these uncertainties without obscuring the measurement process.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Calibration Record

Each instrument should maintain a calibration record containing:

- instrument revision;
- builder;
- construction date;
- calibration date;
- measured fiducial dimensions;
- repeatability estimates;
- known limitations.

Calibration should be repeated whenever significant modifications are made.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Acceptance Criteria

An instrument should be considered suitable for scientific observations when:

- measurements are repeatable;
- no significant mechanical defects remain;
- calibration results are documented;
- known limitations are understood.

Perfect accuracy is not required.

Documented uncertainty is far more valuable than assumed precision.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Future Development

Future versions of this protocol may include:

- printed calibration targets;
- standardized terrestrial validation procedures;
- statistical analysis of repeatability;
- calibration software;
- community calibration datasets.

The calibration protocol is expected to evolve alongside the instrument itself.
