# Mastery

The Kepler project is founded upon a simple principle:

> **Scientific mastery grows by progressively understanding—and eventually improving—the tools used to investigate the natural world.**

The purpose of a Kepler instrument is therefore not merely to produce measurements.

It is to help participants understand how those measurements are created.

Every step between observing the sky and recording a scientific measurement should be understandable to the participant. Whenever practical, the instrument should make its underlying measurement principles visible rather than hiding them behind automation or unexplained mechanisms.

This philosophy extends throughout the Kepler project.

Participants may initially learn to use an instrument designed by others.

With experience they may learn to calibrate it.

Later they may understand the geometry underlying its operation.

Eventually they may improve the design itself or contribute entirely new instruments to the project.

Building an instrument is therefore not simply a practical exercise.

It is one stage in the development of scientific mastery.

The Kepler Cross-staff is intended to be the first instrument in this progression.

Participants begin by measuring the heavens.

Over time they learn not only to make better measurements, but to understand why those measurements work and how they might be improved for those who follow.

In this way, every participant contributes not only observations, but also to the continuing evolution of the Kepler Observatory itself.
