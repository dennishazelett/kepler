# FND-003 — The Journey of Ra

### Following the Sun

## Summary

The Sun is the most regular object in the sky. The ancient Egyptians believed that the sun god Ra sailed across the heavens each day in his solar barque, bringing light to the world before journeying through the underworld each night to be reborn at dawn. This investigation follows the Sun's own annual journey through the sky—not through myth, but through careful observation.

Unlike the Moon, whose changes are rapid and immediately apparent, the Sun changes slowly. Its path across the sky evolves almost imperceptibly from day to day, requiring careful observation over weeks, months, and years before its motion becomes fully apparent.

This investigation invites participants to build an empirical record of the Sun's motion through their own sky. Rather than relying on published ephemerides or diagrams, participants gradually construct their own understanding through repeated observations.

Because the Sun's annual motion never repeats exactly within a single observing season, this investigation naturally continues throughout the participant's involvement with Kepler.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Scientific Question

> **How does the apparent path of the Sun change throughout the year?**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Objectives

By completing this investigation, participants should develop experience in:

- making long-term, consistent observations;
- recognizing gradual change through accumulated evidence;
- using a gnomon to make repeatable measurements;
- identifying local solar noon empirically;
- distinguishing observations from inferred models;
- building confidence in interpreting longitudinal datasets.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Investigation

Observe the Sun regularly over the course of the year.

Observations may be made using shadows cast by a gnomon, projected solar images, or other approved Kepler observation methods.

**The Sun must never be viewed directly without appropriate and approved solar observing techniques.**

Participants should establish an observation routine that can be sustained over long periods. Missing observations are expected and should simply be documented rather than reconstructed.

The objective is not to create a perfect record, but an honest one.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Suggested Observations

Examples of observations include:

- date and time;
- observing location;
- observing conditions;
- gnomon shadow length;
- gnomon shadow direction;
- estimated time of local solar noon;
- apparent position of the Sun relative to the Observer's Atlas;
- seasonal notes;
- optional angular measurements using approved Kepler instruments.

These observations are suggestions rather than requirements.

Investigators should record whatever observations they consider scientifically useful.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Scientific Products

This investigation contributes permanently to the participant's scientific record.

Products may include:

- a longitudinal solar observing log;
- a multi-year record of gnomon measurements;
- estimates of local solar noon throughout the year;
- continuing additions to the Observer's Atlas;
- an increasingly refined empirical estimate of the Sun's annual path through the participant's personal sky.

Unlike a published ephemeris, this estimate is constructed entirely from the participant's own observations. As additional years of data accumulate, confidence in the inferred path increases and the observational record becomes progressively more valuable.

The primary scientific record remains the observations themselves. The inferred solar path is a model derived from those observations and should continue to be refined as new evidence is collected.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Reflection

At the conclusion of each observing season, review the accumulated observations.

Consider questions such as:

- Which changes were obvious only after many weeks or months?
- Which observations appear most repeatable?
- Which measurements appear most uncertain?
- How has the inferred solar path changed as additional observations were collected?
- What observations would improve next year's record?

Scientific understanding emerges from the accumulation of evidence rather than from any single observation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Relationship to Other Investigations

This investigation develops skills that support many later studies, including celestial coordinate systems, seasonal astronomy, instrument calibration, and historical claim investigations.

The observational record produced here also becomes a permanent component of the Observer's Atlas. Each additional year of observations refines the participant's empirical model of the Sun's motion while preserving the original observations from which that model was inferred.
