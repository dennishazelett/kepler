# Investigations

Investigations are the fundamental unit of the Kepler scientific program.

Each investigation begins with observations of the natural world and culminates in scientific reasoning supported by evidence. Together, the investigations in this repository provide opportunities both to develop scientific practice and to apply it to meaningful scientific questions.

Kepler recognizes two complementary categories of investigation.

## 1. Foundational investigations

Foundational investigations are guided observational studies designed to develop scientific intuition about the sky.

Rather than asking participants to evaluate major scientific claims immediately, these investigations help them build the skills needed for successful independent inquiry. Participants learn to make careful observations, recognize patterns, reason about uncertainty, and develop confidence in collecting and interpreting evidence.

Examples include:

- creating a personal Observer's Atlas;
- following the annual motion of the Sun;
- tracking the Moon through a complete lunation;
- locating and following the visible planets;
- measuring planetary elongation;
- observing retrograde motion.

As participants complete foundational investigations they gradually build:

- observational skill;
- scientific judgment;
- a personal archive of carefully documented observations.

These become valuable resources for future investigations.

## 2. Claim investigations

Claim investigations invite participants to evaluate historically significant scientific claims.

Unlike foundational investigations, claim investigations intentionally avoid prescribing an observational strategy, analytical method, or scientific argument. Each claim sheet establishes the historical context and the scientific question, while leaving investigators free to decide how best to collect evidence, analyze observations, and communicate their conclusions.

Different investigators may legitimately reach different conclusions based on the observations they collect, the methods they employ, and the assumptions they choose to examine. This freedom is an intentional part of the educational philosophy of Kepler.

## Relationship between the two

Foundational and claim investigations are complementary rather than sequential.

Participants are free to begin with any investigation that interests them. However, foundational investigations provide an effective bridge to more open-ended scientific inquiry by developing observational skill, scientific judgment, and a personal body of observations that can later support claim investigations.

Investigators who have completed several foundational investigations will often find that claim investigations require less preliminary work because they already possess both the experience and the evidence needed to evaluate scientific questions.

Other investigators may choose to begin directly with a claim investigation, acquiring these skills and observations as part of the investigation itself. Kepler supports both approaches.

## Investigations and the rest of the repository

Investigations define the scientific questions being explored.

Other parts of the repository provide the tools needed to pursue those questions:

- instrument designs make observations possible;
- calibration procedures establish measurement quality;
- observation protocols promote consistency;
- data standards preserve observations for future use;
- software supports analysis and visualization.

Foundational investigations often recommend these resources as part of a guided scientific experience. Claim investigations may draw upon the same resources but intentionally do not prescribe how they should be used.

The goal is to standardize measurements—not scientific reasoning.
