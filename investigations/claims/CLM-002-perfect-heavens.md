# Claim Sheet

**Identifier:** CLM-002\
**Title:** The Heavens Are Perfect\
**Status:** Draft

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Scientific Claim

> The motions of the planets are governed by perfect geometric order.

Your objective is **not** to discover a better model.

Your objective is to determine whether this claim continues to account for the observations available to you.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Historical Reflection

*Johannes Kepler did not begin his scientific career searching for elliptical orbits. He believed that the universe had been constructed according to perfect geometric principles. In his 1596 work,* Mysterium Cosmographicum, *he proposed that the six known planetary orbits were determined by the five Platonic solids nested between concentric spheres. To Kepler, this was not merely an elegant mathematical idea—it reflected the underlying harmony of creation.*

*When Kepler inherited Tycho Brahe's extraordinarily precise observations of Mars, he expected them to confirm this vision. Instead, he encountered persistent discrepancies between prediction and observation. The differences were small, but they refused to disappear.*

*Kepler could have dismissed the observations as imperfect. Instead, he chose to question his own model. That decision ultimately transformed astronomy.*

*This investigation invites you to confront the same dilemma. Begin not by searching for a better explanation, but by asking whether your current explanation continues to earn your confidence.*

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Scientific Background

Scientific revolutions rarely begin with a new theory.

They begin when careful observations refuse to fit an existing one.

Your task is therefore not to attack the claim under investigation, nor to defend it.

Your task is to determine whether its predictions remain consistent with observation.

Only if they do not should alternative explanations become worthy of future investigation.

# Ground Rules

1.  Clearly define the geometric model you are evaluating.

2.  State the predictions your model makes before comparing them with observations.

3.  Distinguish carefully between:

    - observations;
    - assumptions;
    - model predictions;
    - interpretations.

4.  Preserve primary observations without modification.

5.  Record measurement uncertainty whenever possible.

6.  Make your reasoning transparent enough that another investigator could critique or reproduce it.

7.  Conclusions should be proportional to the available evidence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# The Challenge

Design your own investigation.

Consider questions such as:

- If the claim were an accurate description of planetary motion, what observations would you expect to make?
- Which of those predictions can you test using Kepler observations?
- Which observations agree with your predictions?
- Which observations appear inconsistent with them?
- Are those inconsistencies large enough to matter?
- Could they reasonably be explained by observational uncertainty?
- If the claim no longer provides an adequate explanation, what alternative models deserve further investigation?

These questions are suggestions rather than requirements.

There is no prescribed experimental procedure.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Deliverable

Produce a scientific paper that includes:

- the claim under investigation;
- the model you constructed;
- the predictions made by that model;
- the observations collected;
- the methods employed;
- your comparison between prediction and observation;
- uncertainties and limitations;
- the conclusions you believe are justified by the available evidence.

Your objective is not to produce the correct model.

Your objective is to produce the strongest scientific argument your observations can support.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Reflection

Science advances when investigators are willing to compare their expectations honestly with the evidence.

Before considering your investigation complete, reflect on the following.

- Which assumptions in your model proved most reliable?
- Which assumptions were challenged?
- Which observations most influenced your conclusions?
- What evidence would most change your mind?
- If your model appears inadequate, what qualities should a better model possess?

Resist the temptation to replace one model with another too quickly.

A scientific model is not rejected because a more attractive idea exists.

It is rejected only when it no longer accounts for the evidence.

# Epilogue

Many histories of science celebrate Johannes Kepler because he discovered that planets move in elliptical orbits.

His greater achievement was something more fundamental.

He refused to ignore observations simply because they disagreed with a beautiful idea.

As Kepler later reflected (from *Astronomia Nova*):

> *Divine Providence granted us such a diligent observer in Tycho Brahe that his observations convicted this calculation of an error of eight minutes. It is only right that we should accept this gift with gratitude and put it to use. If I had believed that we could ignore these eight minutes [of arc], I would have patched up my hypothesis accordingly. But, since it was not permissible to ignore, those eight minutes pointed the road to a complete reformation in astronomy.*

Those "eight minutes" remind us that scientific progress often begins not with dramatic discoveries, but with the decision to take inconvenient evidence seriously.
